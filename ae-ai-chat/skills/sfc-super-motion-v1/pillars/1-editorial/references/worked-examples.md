# Three worked editing decisions

All cases below are **fictional exercises created for this skill**, not analyses of inspected video. Behavior, coverage, and briefs are supplied facts within each exercise. No timecode or fps is supplied, so choices use beat descriptions; exact frames must be set against real footage.

## 1. Dialogue: a reaction conflicts with a musical beat

**Fictional brief.** A drama should move the audience from worry to relief when Linh tells her mother she has received a scholarship. Coverage includes A: Linh speaking; B: her mother listening, inhaling, then smiling; C: both together. B belongs to the same exchange. Temp music has a strong beat before the smile develops in B.

**Option A.** Cut to C on the musical accent as the announcement ends. Picture and music share a clear emphasis, and the relationship is visible. The expected cost is losing the mother's process of receiving the news; viewers know the outcome without experiencing the reaction.

**Option B.** Once the scholarship information has landed, move picture to B while A's final words continue. Hold until the reaction becomes readable, then consider C. This uses an L-cut and may require adjusting the temp music.

**Provisional choice.** Test B first because the brief centers the transition to relief. This is a prediction from the supplied description, not a playback-verified result. Do not prolong B merely to make it more emotional once the smile is clear and nothing further develops.

| Criterion | Assessment from the fictional facts |
|---|---|
| Emotion | B shows the recipient's change, which appears appropriate to the intended experience. |
| Story | The scholarship information survives, with an added consequence for the mother. |
| Rhythm | Prioritize the reception beat over the temp-music beat; actual audiovisual timing needs review. |
| Eye-trace | Frame positions are unknown; check recognition of the mother on entry to B. |
| 2D plane | Axis/eyeline details are missing; screen continuity cannot yet be confirmed. |
| 3D space | The brief supplies the relationship; actual shot states still need inspection. |

**Verification.** Review A→B→C with dialogue and music; compare the beat-led version; check the reaction's genuine context, overlap quality, and development. If this were a factual interview and B came from another question, do not present it as the real scholarship reaction. Retain A, choose truthful coverage, or preserve an actual pause instead.

**Example concise response.** “Try moving to the mother after the key information and carrying Linh's voice over the reaction. This may clarify relief, at the cost of adjusting the musical accent. Without footage, eye-trace, axis continuity, and exact timing remain unverified.”

## 2. Action: screen direction reverses

**Fictional brief.** Viewers must understand Minh chasing Huy toward the door at the end of a corridor. A shows both running left to right. B is shot from across the axis: Minh runs right to left on screen while continuing toward the same destination in the scene. C contains a visible camera move across the axis, retaining both people and the destination. D is an insert of the door handle without showing their positions.

**Option A.** Join A→B because B has the strongest performance. This supports action energy, but the reversal may suggest Minh turns back or runs toward Huy. Distinguish the layers: the path can remain continuous in 3D while its 2D projection reverses.

**Option B.** Use A→C, then B from the established new side, if C is clear and maintains useful pace. A visible crossing is a candidate for introducing the new viewpoint. ([Lehigh's crossing-the-line guidance](https://lehigh.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LKB/pages/26682755/Crossing%2Bthe%2BLine%2B-%2BVideo%2BBasics))

**Option C.** Insert D and assume it automatically fixes the axis reversal. That conclusion lacks evidence: D conceals a join but does not necessarily re-establish people, direction, or destination. It can be useful if the handle matters to the story, while orientation still needs checking.

**Provisional choice.** Test A→C→B because the brief prioritizes a legible chase destination. Compare it with A→B in playback; C could be too long or distracting. If C does not help, consider same-side coverage if available. Mirroring B is not the default fix; inspect text, handedness, props, and directional meaning first.

| Layer | Distinction to check |
|---|---|
| Eye-trace | Can viewers find the correct pursuer/target when B begins? Frame locations and competitors are not supplied. |
| 2D plane | Left-to-right changes to right-to-left; this is a provided exercise fact. |
| 3D space | Destination and pursuit remain continuous in the description; action phase, distance, and positions need real-footage inspection. |
| Emotion/story/rhythm | Strong performance matters only if the necessary action relationship remains understandable; C must also serve the chase's rhythm. |

**Intentional variant.** If the brief intends to convey Minh's disorientation, A→B may be worth testing. Specify what viewers must still understand, where confusion is intended, and which coverage will restore orientation. “Break the axis to look cinematic” is not a sufficiently concrete purpose.

**Verification.** Watch the passage before and after the door rather than the join alone. Ask a test viewer who is chasing whom and where they are going. Keep or change the choice based on this scene's result; C is not a guaranteed repair for every crossing.

## 3. Vertical tutorial: captions, action, and crop compete

**Fictional brief.** A product tutorial explains how to lock a latch, delivering 9:16 from horizontal footage. In A, the presenter's face is left and the manipulating hand lower right. B is a close-up of the turning step; C shows the locked result. An automatic face-centered crop removes the hand from A. A long caption covers the latch at the start of B.

**Option A.** Retain the centered face and cut quickly A→C, letting text replace the turning step. The result is brief and facially clear. The cost is losing the action viewers need to learn; text may not communicate the movement adequately.

**Option B.** Reframe to retain the hand–product relationship where source framing permits; show B for the operation; place captions in usable space or divide them meaningfully; retain C to confirm the result. A's explanation can continue over B if the meaning fits.

**Provisional choice.** Test B because successful instruction is the goal. Do not invent a fixed duration or safe-area preset. If the crop cannot retain both hand and face, prioritize the current beat's information, use existing close coverage, or clearly identify a pickup needed.

| Criterion | Expected effect |
|---|---|
| Emotion | Clear instruction and a successful result can support confidence without forcing a dramatic climax. |
| Story | Preserve the turning step and evidence of completion. |
| Rhythm | Follow explanation, operation, and recognition of the result; test caption reception in playback. |
| Eye-trace | Guide from hand to latch; avoid covering or competing with the crucial operation. |
| 2D plane | Check that cropping preserves readable eyelines/interactions and operation direction. |
| 3D space | Retain before/during/after latch states and the hand's relationship to the mechanism. |

**Verification.** Review the 9:16 output with sound and captions at the intended viewing size. Ask a test viewer how to turn the latch and how to recognize success. Preserve necessary captions while adjusting information distribution. With only a transcript, this remains a coverage plan/checklist rather than a confirmed fault in an actual crop or caption layout.

## When only a transcript is available

From “I turn the latch, then check it is locked,” the AI can propose operation and confirmation beats. It cannot invent a lower-right hand, camera axis, or reaction duration. An appropriate response is: “Coverage is needed for the turn and the locked state. Without images, eye-trace/2D/3D have insufficient evidence. Meaning-based cut suggestions are provisional; inspect sound and movement before finalizing.”
