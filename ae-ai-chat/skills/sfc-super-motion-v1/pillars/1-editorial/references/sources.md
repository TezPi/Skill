# Research sources and limitations

Synthesized on **28 October 2026**, Asia/Bangkok. The skill uses the 15 sources below. Original definitions and craft perspectives, cognition research, and the skill's application workflow are distinguished. None of these sources establishes automatic coefficients for selecting an optimal cut.

## Rule of Six foundations

| Source | Use and scope |
|---|---|
| [Silman-James Press — In the Blink of an Eye, 2nd Edition](https://www.silmanjamespress.com/shop/filmmaking-directing/in-the-blink-of-an-eye2nd-edition/) | The publisher confirms the book, Walter Murch's authorship, and ISBN 9781879505629. This product page does not provide the full Rule of Six chapter. |
| [UC Berkeley — The Rule of Six, Walter Murch](https://blogs.ischool.berkeley.edu/i290-viznarr-s12/the-rule-of-six-walter-murch/) | A 2012 course page preserves an excerpt of Murch's text containing the six criteria, order, and 51/23/10/7/5/4 figures. It is an archived author excerpt, not a publisher-hosted chapter or verification of every edition. |
| [Steve Hullfish interviews Walter Murch — Art of the Cut, 2019](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-books/) | The author explains the illustrative numbers, the link between 2D continuity and the 180-degree rule, and hierarchical tradeoffs. This is craft testimony, not an experimental validation of weights. |

## Editing practice and technical terminology

| Source | Use and scope |
|---|---|
| [Victor Wishalla interviews Murch — Cutting from the Heart, MovieMaker](https://www.moviemaker.com/walter-murch-cutting-from-the-heart-3217/) | Originally published 4 December 1998; page updated 31 January 2023. Supports discussion of picture changes following meaning and reaction in dialogue; supplies no mandatory frame duration. |
| [Joseph “Jay” Duerr interviews Murch — Making Visual Music, CineMontage, 1 May 2004](https://cinemontage.org/visual-music-walter-murch/) | Discusses performance/scene rhythm and testing visual/dramatic structure before temp music. | [Karen Pearlman — Keeping Emotions Moving, 7 October 2012](https://www.karenpearlman.net/karenpearlmannet/keeping-emotions-moving.html) | The author's account of performance beats, changes in tactics, and observable cues. It does not establish actual inner states from an expression. |

| [Columbia Film Language Glossary — 180-Degree Rule](https://filmglossary.ccnmtl.columbia.edu/term/180-degree-rule/) | Official educational explanation of screen orientation and deliberate convention-breaking. This is film-language instruction rather than a psychological law. |
| [CCEA — Moving Image Arts, Teacher Guidance, 2019](https://ccea.org.uk/downloads/docs/Support/General/2019/Moving%20Image%20Arts%20-%20Teacher%20Guidance.pdf) | Printed pages 20–21, PDF pages 23–24: eyeline match, match-on-action, and continuity tools. Their effects can overlap across criteria. |
| [Filmmakers Academy — Continuity Editing](https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/glossary/continuity-editing/) | Professional educational definition of continuity techniques, not a checklist requiring every technique in every scene. |
| [Lehigh University — Crossing the Line, Video Basics](https://lehigh.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/LKB/pages/26682755/Crossing%2Bthe%2BLine%2B-%2BVideo%2BBasics) | Describes a visible camera crossing as a way to introduce a new side. This is a candidate to test, not a guaranteed repair for all disorientation. |

## Original cognition research

| Source | Finding used and limits |
|---|---|
| [Tim J. Smith — The Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity, 2012, author repository](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/) | An attentional model with eye-tracking illustrations; [author manuscript PDF](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/2/6679.pdf). Supports considering cues and expectations, without guaranteeing identical gaze or understanding across viewers. |
| [Joseph P. Magliano & Jeffrey M. Zacks — The Impact of Continuity Editing in Narrative Film on Event Segmentation, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3208769/) | DOI 10.1111/j.1551-6709.2011.01202.x. In data using *The Red Balloon*, action discontinuity related more strongly to event boundaries than spatial/temporal changes. One film and this design do not cover every technique or genre. |
| [James E. Cutting — Sequences in Popular Cinema Generate Inconsistent Event Segmentation, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6675763/) | DOI 10.3758/s13414-019-01757-w. Analysis of 24 films from 1940–2010; links between music, shot-duration patterns, and sequence segmentation are correlational. It does not measure social-platform retention or prove faster cutting is better. |
| [Joy & Padakannaya — Effect of Subtitles on Gaze Behavior during Shot Changes, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10723748/) | Eye tracking with 25 students viewing English clips and English subtitles. Supports treating subtitles as participants in attention allocation, not universal caption presets or removing accessibility captions. |

## What the skill itself contributes

The before/change/after beat map, evidence categories, review statuses, multi-scale workflow, JSON template, and three fictional examples are this skill's application design. They make editorial decisions checkable and transferable. Do not present them as Murch's inventions or an empirically validated model.

Guidance on vertical output, cropping, captions, and graphics applies attention/orientation questions to modern delivery formats. The cited research does not establish a fixed hook duration, gaze-jump threshold, BPM, retention formula, or universal platform safe area.

This research did not inspect the entire book through a publisher preview, verify every edition, or examine a particular user video. Project evidence and real playback determine what can be claimed during application. Sources are concisely paraphrased; the package contains no scans or reproduced book chapter.
